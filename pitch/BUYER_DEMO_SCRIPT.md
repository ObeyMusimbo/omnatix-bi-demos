# Omnatix buyer demo script

For a first meeting with a CFO, COO, CRO or group finance director. Twenty minutes, one demo,
one ask. The goal of the meeting is not to explain Omnatix. It is to book a Discovery Sprint on
their own data.

The full reveal for each demo, with every figure and the questions a technical buyer asks, is
in that project's `ANSWER_KEY.md`. This file is the conversation around it.

---

## The rules

1. **One demo, matched to their industry.** Never tour all four. Range is for investors; depth
   is for buyers.
2. **Open with the gap, then stop talking.** The headline is a number nobody in the room can
   explain. Say it, and wait five seconds.
3. **Three findings, not six.** Pick the three that land on the person in front of you. The
   rest are for the second meeting.
4. **Every finding ends on "now what".** The AI panel does this for you: point at the actions
   and the owner, not only the number.
5. **Ask more than you show.** The last third of the meeting is their business, not your demo.
6. **Close on the sprint, with a date.** "Shall we look at yours?" and then a calendar.

---

## The twenty minutes

| Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | Permission: "I'll show you about twelve minutes of what we found in a business like yours, then ask you three questions about yours. Fair?" |
| 2 to 3 | The gap. Say the headline. Stop. |
| 3 to 9 | Three findings, each with its AI panel |
| 9 to 11 | Trust: the data quality the pipeline fixed, and how the AI shows its working |
| 11 to 12 | What it is worth: the total |
| 12 to 17 | Their business: the discovery questions |
| 17 to 20 | The offer and a date |

## Meridian Provisions: FMCG distribution, for a CFO or Commercial Director

**Open on:** the front page. Light mode.

> "Revenue up 11.9%. Gross profit up 4.1%. That gap is R12.6 million, in one year."

Pause. Then: "Here is where it went."

**Three moments, pick by the buyer:**

- **Commercial Director: The discount nobody reset.** Scroll to chapter 03.
  > "Their largest account drifted from 8.1% discount to 15.3% over two years. Some of that is
  > the market. The rest of it is this account alone. Nobody ever reset it, because revenue kept
  > growing. That is R3.9 million of revenue in twelve months."
- **CFO: The range sold at a loss.** Chapter 01.
  > "Bottled water, 12.9% gross margin, looks fine on a product report. Allocate the freight by
  > weight and the rebate by revenue, and it loses R3.8 million a year. The costs sit at order
  > level in the finance export, so no product report has ever seen them."
- **Trade marketing or Sales: The promotion.** Chapter 02.
  > "Buy 2 Get 1 Free on their laundry powder. Volume triples, which is why everyone thinks it
  > works. Every case on deal loses R18, and for four weeks afterwards the line sells at 45% of
  > normal. The volume was borrowed from next month."
- **Operations: Weekend stock-outs.** Chapter 04. "Sales that never happened do not appear in
  any sales report. R1.1 million of them did not happen here."

**The AI moment:** point at the Analyst's note in the chapter you just showed.

> "Every night an AI analyst writes this from the same tables: what happened, why nobody saw it,
> the actions, the owner and the rand value. And it tells you which model wrote it."

**The question to ask them:** "When did someone last look at realised discount by account, not
list price? Who owns that number in your business?"

## Kestrel Logistics: road freight, for a COO or MD

**Open on:** the Network view. Dark mode.

> "Nearly a quarter of every kilometre this fleet ran last year carried nothing. Until now nobody
> was charged for it."

**Three moments:**

- **Empty returns.** Click the map's red corridors, then the Empty returns view.
  > "Every lane was priced assuming 60% of the return would sell. On Johannesburg to Cape Town,
  > their biggest lane, 20% does. It looks like R8.8 million of contribution. Charged for the
  > empty truck home, it loses R1.8 million."
- **Redeliveries.** "3,334 drops refused on first attempt, R6.5 million in journeys that earned
  nothing. Six sites cause most of it, and most of those have no booked receiving slot. That is
  six phone calls, not an analysis."
- **Friday.** "On time is 95.8% on a normal day, 74.1% on a Friday and 65.2% on the month end
  run. It lands hardest on the accounts that pay most for service, and the service clause on the
  anchor account turned it into R5.2 million of penalties."
- For a fleet manager, swap in **Fuel**: "Seven trucks burning 18% to 30% more diesel than their
  own class on the same work. R2.5 million a year. Worth a workshop booking to find out why."

**The AI moment:** the alerts rail on the right, then the recommendation strip on any view.

> "The rail is the exceptions, ranked. The strip under each view is the AI's recommendation, as
> a checklist with owners. A control room, not a report."

**Close the demo on:** What it is worth. "R19.7 million a year on R27.6 million earned. Same
fleet, same customers."

**The question to ask them:** "How do you price a lane today, and when did you last check the
backhaul you assumed against what you actually got?"

## Sable & Finch Credit: microfinance, for a CRO or the credit committee

**Open on:** the cover page. Light mode. Turn pages with the arrow keys.

> "The same product, written a year apart, goes bad much faster now: the month six bad rate has
> moved from about 6.9% to about 10.9%. And the arrears ratio the board sees has barely moved.
> A growing book hides it."

**Three moments:**

- **The branch.** Page 3.
  > "One branch runs at 1.62 times the book's bad rate. The column beside it is the one that
  > matters: declared income exceeds the income on file on 63.7% of its loans, and on none
  > anywhere else. That is not a risk appetite conversation. It is a conduct one, and it was
  > visible before the first instalment."
- **Affordability.** Page 4.
  > "R34.8 million is outstanding on 3,201 agreements written below the affordability floor. A
  > court can set those aside as reckless credit under sections 81 and 83 of the National Credit
  > Act. That is not an expected loss, it is a legal exposure, which is why it sits in its own
  > box and is never added to the loss."
- **Top-ups.** Page 5. "849 of 852 top-ups settled an account already in arrears. The old loan
  closes as Settled, the new one opens as Current, and every report improves."
- For operations, swap in **Debit orders**: "First instalments fail 17% of the time when the
  debit order runs a week after payday, against 8% on payday. It is a field on a form."

**The AI moment:** the Recommendation to the committee at the foot of any page, then the last
page.

> "Every matter closes on a recommendation, set out the way a board paper would be. The last
> page is the draft resolutions with owners and signature lines. The committee decides; the pack
> arrives ready to decide."

**The question to ask them:** "Do you see vintage curves in your credit committee pack today, or
portfolio at risk?"

## Lumen Health Network: private clinics, for a group MD or finance director

**Open on:** the Overview tab. Light mode.

> "The group billed R160.8 million for care it delivered and collected R143.4 million. R17.4
> million never arrived. Nothing was stolen and nobody was negligent."

**Three moments:**

- **Claims.** "R11 million of rejected claims were never recovered. Two clinics recover one
  rejection in twenty, the other four one in four, with the same schemes and the same software.
  R6.3 million of it is a coding or referral fix at the practice."
- **The week.** Point at the heat grid on the overview.
  > "Monday at eight, the rooms are 17% full. Friday at four, 92% full with a 33 minute wait.
  > Capacity is not short. It is in the wrong place."
  Then say the line that earns trust: "That empty time is a cost, not lost revenue, so we never
  add it to the R17.4 million. Anyone who adds them is wrong in both directions."
- **No-shows.** "Half of all bookings never got a reminder SMS. On this practice's own numbers,
  reminders would recover about 11,000 visits worth R9.5 million."
- For a practice manager, swap in **Reception**: "Self funded patients pay 89% at the desk.
  Scheme patients pay 50% of the small gap. Same desk, same card machine."

**The AI moment:** the Decision support pane beside any tab.

> "The analysis on the left, the decision on the right, and it stays in view as you scroll.
> Operational, never clinical."

**The question to ask them:** "Who works your rejected claims today, and how would you know if
nobody did for a month?"

## The trust segment (two minutes, every demo)

Buyers do not fear the dashboard. They fear acting on a wrong number. Show them why they will
not.

- **The data quality panel or list.** "Your exports have duplicates, mixed date formats and
  broken references. Everyone's do. The pipeline fixes each one visibly, tests it on every
  build, and if a test fails nothing is published."
- **The AI's working.** "The AI writes from the figures on this page and names itself. Every
  rand value it quotes is checked against the data before it is published."
- **Where the data goes.** "The dashboard runs in your browser. Your detailed records do not sit
  on our servers."

---

## The discovery questions (five minutes)

Ask these, write the answers down, and read them back before you make the offer.

1. "Which number in your business do you trust least right now?"
2. "What does your team export every month, and from which systems?"
3. "When the board or the owner asks why profit moved, how long does it take to answer, and who
   does it?"
4. "If we found one thing worth a million rand in your data, who would own fixing it?"
5. "What have you tried before? BI tools, consultants, an analyst? What happened?"

## The offer

> "Here is what I suggest. A three week Discovery Sprint on your own exports. You send the same
> files you already produce. In three weeks you get the findings, each with a rand value and an
> owner, and the live dashboard. The fee is **[R__]**, fixed.
>
> If you then want it kept live, rebuilt and checked every night with the AI analyst and a
> monthly review, that is the subscription at **[R__]** a month. If not, you keep the findings."

**Risk reversal, if you choose to offer it:** "If the sprint does not find at least **[five]**
times its fee in recoverable value, you do not pay for it." Decide this before the meeting and
offer it the same way every time. It removes the reason to delay.

Then: "Could we start on the **[date]**? I will send the list of exports today."

## Objections

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We already have Power BI." | "Good, keep it. Power BI shows the numbers you give it. We find the ones that are not in any report yet, like the empty truck home or the claim nobody reworked, and the results can sit in your Power BI too." |
| "Our data is too messy." | "Everyone's is. The demos have mixed date formats, duplicates and broken references on purpose, and the pipeline fixes and tests each one. Messy data is the job." |
| "Send me something." | "I will. What would make it worth your time to read? The sprint outline, or this demo as a PDF with the numbers?" Then book the follow-up before you hang up. |
| "No budget this year." | "The sprint is sized so one finding pays for it. If we find nothing worth several times the fee, [you do not pay]. Is budget the real question, or is it whether we will find something?" |
| "We have an analyst." | "Then they will love this. We give them a tested warehouse and a method, so they spend their time acting on findings instead of building joins." |
| "Is our data safe? POPIA?" | "The dashboard runs in your browser, and we work from aggregated tables, not personal records. We will put the data flow in writing before we start." |
| "How is this different from our auditors?" | "Your auditors check that the numbers are right. We look for money the numbers are hiding. Different question." |
| "AI makes things up." | "Which is why ours only writes from the figures on the page, names itself, and has every rand value checked against the data before it is published. The warehouse decides the numbers. The AI explains them." |
| "We'll think about it." | "Of course. What would you need to see to say yes? Let's put fifteen minutes in the diary for [date] to decide either way." |
